Buying or selling a home is a significant event that involves multiple legal steps and documentation. Whether you're purchasing your first property or moving on from a beloved family home, navigating the complexities of a real estate transaction in Ontario can be challenging. This article outlines the legal steps involved for both buyers and sellers, explaining key terms and procedures to demystify the process.

## Legal Steps When Buying a Home

When buying a home in Ontario, there are several crucial legal steps involved. As a buyer, you will first need to make an offer by signing an Agreement of Purchase and Sale. This document sets out the terms of your agreement with the seller, including the price, deposit amount, closing date, and any conditions that you want to include, such as a home inspection.

Once the offer is accepted, your next step is often to arrange financing and insurance, if necessary. Simultaneously, your [real estate lawyer](/practice/real-estate) will conduct a title search. This process ensures that the property is legally owned by the seller, free from liens or claims from third parties. Any issues must be resolved before closing.

Another factor to consider is the statement of adjustments. This document details financial adjustments for things like property taxes or utilities that need to be settled at closing. Once all conditions are met, the funds are transferred, and you receive the keys to your new home.

## Legal Steps When Selling a Home

Selling a property also involves a series of legal procedures. First, you must list your property, typically with a real estate agent who can market the home and facilitate showings. Accepting an offer starts with reviewing the Agreement of Purchase and Sale the buyer submits. It's essential to ensure that the terms align with your needs.

Your lawyer will handle the payment of funds from the sale, paying off any existing mortgage, and transferring title to the buyer. A seller may also need to prepare and execute a series of closing documents, including the deed. Understanding these documents can prevent last-minute surprises and ensure that everything goes smoothly.

## Key Differences Between Buying and Selling

While both sides of a transaction involve similar documents like the Agreement of Purchase and Sale and the statement of adjustments, the key differences lie in their roles and responsibilities:

- **Title Search**: Critical for buyers, as it confirms the seller's legal right to sell the property.
- **Property Disclosure**: Sellers must provide information on any issues with the property to the buyer.
- **Adjustments & Closing Costs**: Buyers typically cover more upfront costs, whereas sellers need to settle any existing encumbrances like mortgages.

## Common Pitfalls and What to Watch For

Real estate transactions can go awry without careful attention. Common pitfalls include:

- **Title Issues**: Discovering outstanding claims or encumbrances can delay closing.
- **Financing Problems**: Buyers may face challenges obtaining final mortgage approval.
- **Inspection Surprises**: Differences in property condition versus inspection reports can lead to renegotiation.
- **Missing or Incorrect Documents**: Errors in paperwork can delay both sides of the process.

Ensuring complete and correct documentation, as well as clear communication with all parties involved, is crucial to sidestepping these issues.
