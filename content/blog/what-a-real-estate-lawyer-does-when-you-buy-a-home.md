Buying a home is often the largest purchase a person makes, and it turns entirely on the details. Between the accepted offer and the day you get the keys, a lot has to happen correctly and on time. A real estate lawyer's job is to handle that legal work so closing day feels like a formality rather than a scramble. Here is what actually happens, step by step, and where a lawyer protects you along the way.

## Reviewing the agreement of purchase and sale

Everything starts with the agreement of purchase and sale, the contract that sets the price, the closing date, and the conditions. Ideally you involve a lawyer before you sign, or during any conditional period, while there is still room to raise concerns. Our [real estate](/practice/real-estate) lawyers read the agreement closely and explain what you are committing to, including deposit terms, what is included in the sale, and any conditions such as financing or a home inspection.

If you are buying a condominium, there is an extra document to understand: the status certificate, which reports on the condo corporation's finances and rules. Reviewing it early can reveal issues that are far easier to address before the deal becomes firm than after.

## Searching title and protecting your ownership

Once the agreement is firm, the focus shifts to confirming that the seller can actually transfer clear ownership to you. Your lawyer conducts a title search and a set of off-title searches, which can surface things like:

- unpaid property taxes or liens registered against the property,
- easements or rights that let others use part of the land,
- open building permits or work orders, and
- differences between the legal description and what is actually on the ground.

Where appropriate, your lawyer arranges title insurance, a one-time policy that protects you against certain title defects, fraud, and survey problems. The goal of all of this is simple: no surprises about what you own and what comes with it.

## Working with your lender and the numbers

If you are financing the purchase, your lawyer also acts for your mortgage lender. That means reviewing the lender's instructions, preparing the mortgage documents, and making sure the lender's conditions are met so the funds are ready on closing.

Your lawyer then prepares a statement of adjustments, a plain accounting of who owes what on closing. It credits your deposit, adds closing costs like land transfer tax, and prorates prepaid items such as property taxes. You will know the exact amount to bring before closing day, not after.

## Buying through a company or with others

Not every purchase is a couple buying a family home. If you are buying an investment property through a corporation, or a commercial property, the structure matters for liability and tax, and it should be set up correctly from the start. That work overlaps with [corporate structuring](/practice/corporate) and, for commercial deals, the kind of [agreements a business relies on](/practice/business). If several people are buying together, the way you hold title affects what happens if one owner dies or wants out, which is also where thinking ahead about a [will and power of attorney](/practice/wills-estates) fits naturally into the picture.

## What to watch for

A few issues come up again and again, and catching them early saves stress:

- **Signing before review.** Once an agreement is firm, its terms are hard to change. Get advice during the conditional period, not after.
- **A closing date that is too tight.** Financing, searches, and lender funding all take time. Build in a realistic window.
- **Assuming everything is included.** Fixtures, appliances, and chattels should be spelled out in the agreement, not assumed.
- **Last-minute changes to your finances.** New debt or a job change before closing can affect your mortgage approval.

## How a lawyer helps, and what to do next

A real estate lawyer is not just a signer at the end. They read the contract before it binds you, uncover title problems while they can still be fixed, coordinate with your lender so funds arrive on time, and make sure the transfer registers cleanly so you become the legal owner without a hitch.

If you are getting ready to buy, the best time to talk to a lawyer is before you sign, not the week of closing.
